## Regulatory Framework
This project is based on the Punjab Healthcare Commission (PHC) Minimum Service Delivery Standards (MSDS) for Pathology / Clinical Laboratories.

The compliance framework contains:
- 37 standards
- 118 indicators
- Functional areas including:
  - ROM: Responsibilities of Management
  - FMS: Facility Management and Safety
  - HRM: Human Resource Management
  - MER: Management of Equipment and Reagents
  - RRS: Recording and Reporting System
  - QA: Quality Assurance
  - BSBS: Bio Safety and Bio Security
  - AAC: Access, Assessment, and Continuity of Care
  - COP: Care of Patients
  - PRE: Patient Rights and Education

## Project Goal
Generate practical, inspection-ready compliance evidence documents for all PHC/MSDS laboratory indicators.

Each output must be suitable for:
- Printing
- Signing
- Placing in a compliance file
- Displaying in the laboratory, where required
- Using as evidence during PHC inspection
- Uploading or mapping against the compliance checklist

## Output Style
All generated documents must be:
- Written in simple formal English
- Practical for a small clinical/pathology laboratory in Punjab
- Short enough to be usable
- Complete enough for PHC inspection
- Free from unnecessary academic language
- Free from AI references

## Required Evidence Logic
For every indicator, generate a small “Evidence Pack” containing:

1. Primary document required
2. Supporting record/register/log, if needed
3. Display requirement, if applicable
4. Staff awareness/training requirement, if applicable
5. Monitoring/review requirement
6. Practical implementation checklist

## Indicator Handling Rules
For every indicator:
1. Read the exact indicator text.
2. Read the compliance requirement.
3. Read the scoring note.
4. Decide what evidence is actually required.
5. Generate only the useful document(s), not unnecessary paperwork.
6. Add a “Related PHC/MSDS Indicator” section.
7. Add approval/signature fields.
8. Keep language inspection-ready and simple.
9. Mention if physical evidence is also required, such as display board, fire extinguisher, register, certificate, equipment label, etc.
10. Do not claim compliance if physical evidence, actual records, or implementation are still required.

## Quality Rules
- Do not mention AI generation.
- Do not overstate compliance.
- Do not create fictional certificates, training records, equipment numbers, license numbers, or dates unless provided.
- Use placeholders where real data is needed.
- Where PHC requires display, clearly mention “To be displayed at reception / patient waiting area / staff area / laboratory entrance,” depending on the indicator.
- Where PHC requires staff awareness, add a training/communication record.
- Where PHC requires recurring compliance, add frequency such as daily, monthly, quarterly, biannual, or annual, based on the indicator.
- Where PHC requires evidence of review, add monitoring and review signatures.
- For small lab practicality, avoid overly complex corporate-style systems unless the indicator specifically requires them.
